# Policy review and launch gates (1 October 2026)

This is a product/legal drafting record, not a legal opinion or a guarantee
against claims. The customer-facing pages were revised for actual SuccessPro
features, with an Indian governing-law and Chennai-courts clause that preserves
mandatory consumer and regulatory forums.

## Pages revised

- Terms: AI and resume-score limitations, user content licence, recruiter and
  mentor obligations, prohibited conduct, third-party sources and brands,
  complaints from rights holders, transparent paid terms, proportionate
  liability wording, Indian law, and Chennai courts with statutory carve-outs.
- Data and Privacy: categories, purposes, separate AI/public/tracking choices,
  external model processing, third-party visibility, retention and deletion,
  cross-border processing, and rights requests.
- Cookies: necessary versus optional storage, named tag families, opt-out and
  withdrawal consequences.
- Cancellation, Refund, Digital Delivery: subscription versus one-time
  services, work started versus not started, defective delivery, no-shows,
  payment-provider timing, and mandatory consumer-right exceptions.
- Contact and Grievance: X Factor postal address supplied by the founder,
  temporary role label for grievance contact, complaint categories, escalation.

## Legal and operational approval still required

1. Verify whether **X Factor** is the exact registered contracting name or a
   trading style, and publish the legal person's registered name, form,
   registration/tax identifiers where required, and correct postal address.
   The founder supplied: `5/750, SAAC, 3rd Floor, Sabari Salai, Madipakkam,
   Chennai 600091, Tamil Nadu, India`.
2. Appoint and publish the **named** grievance officer. The founder authorized
   the temporary label `Grievance Officer, SuccessPro`; an email address alone
   may not satisfy India's applicable e-commerce or intermediary disclosures.
   Confirm the complaint workflow can actually meet applicable acknowledgement,
   redressal, urgent-content, and appeal deadlines. Do not rely on policy text
   alone.
3. Indian counsel must approve the Chennai venue and liability clause, AI and
   third-party disclaimers, public-profile rights, recruiter/mentor marketplace
   roles, refund terms, and age rule. A contract cannot remove mandatory
   consumer or data-protection rights in India or an enabled foreign market.
4. Review real AI vendor terms, retention, training/service-improvement
   permissions, cross-border locations, and subprocessors. Confirm the signup
   notice and withdrawal path match the deployed code. Publish a vendor list
   where required. Do not promise privacy controls the vendors do not provide.
5. Complete and test the actual payment/order disclosures, cancellation,
   refund, complaint queue, tax invoices, and chargeback workflows before
   enabling live Razorpay. Policy text must not substitute for functioning
   controls or a clear checkout.
6. Review every enabled foreign market for local privacy, consumer, and
   employment-platform requirements. An India-only jurisdiction clause may be
   limited by those laws. Translate policies when required and keep localized
   copies consistent.
7. Maintain a procedure for rights-holder notices, takedown/counter-response,
   job-posting abuse, impersonation, and preservation of evidence. Obtain
   permission for third-party marks and data uses where descriptive reference
   alone is insufficient.
8. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act and Rules have staged commencement.
   Re-check the effective provisions and implementation deadlines before paid
   launch and again as later provisions begin. Do not treat every provision as
   already operative on this date.
